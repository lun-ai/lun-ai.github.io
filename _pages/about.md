I am a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Fellow at EMBL's European Bioinformatics Institute ([EMBL-EBI](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/)) in Cambridge, UK, working with [Julio Saez-Rodriguez](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/people/person/julio-saez-rodriguez/). I received my PhD from Imperial College London, supervised by Stephen Muggleton.

I study how to monitor and control LLM agents, using scientific discovery in biology as the testbed. My research focuses on: (1) evaluating and controlling LLM agents in knowledge-intensive workflows, including when they game their evaluations and how external judges can govern them, and (2) whether model explanations faithfully convey what a model knows to a human overseer.

`Working with agents`:
I use coding agents every day in my research, and I keep experimenting with how to work with them better. When a way of working holds up, I package it as an open skill that anyone can drop into Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI. Two I use most:
- Want to learn a technical concept without the hassle? [Mental gym](https://github.com/lun-ai/mental-gym) lets you learn it by building it from scratch. The agent coaches you through a checklist one item at a time without writing the solution for you: it scaffolds a skeleton with hints, you fill the blanks, it critiques your attempt, then asks a depth question to check you really understood.
- Juggling many open research questions across many sessions? [Research dashboard](https://github.com/lun-ai/skills/blob/main/research-dashboard/SKILL.md) keeps them in one place. The agent groups questions into areas, keeps one dashboard plus a plan file per area with effort estimates, and writes every result back into both, so findings outlive the chat.

The rest are in [lun-ai/skills](https://github.com/lun-ai/skills). Feedback and ideas are welcome.
